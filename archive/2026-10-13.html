Science7/10

The first nuclear clock that runs on its own is ticking in Vienna

Why it matters: TU Wien’s thorium-229 crystal clock ran self-stabilized for over 24 hours at instability near 10⁻¹⁵ per day, published in Nature on October 7. It still trails cesium and optical clocks — but it’s the first timepiece regulated by an atomic nucleus rather than its electron shell.

What’s next: a Beijing group has a competing thorium prototype; a stability race is on.
