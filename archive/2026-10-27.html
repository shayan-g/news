Webb reads the ice of an interstellar comet — and it looks strangely familiar

Why it matters: A study published Monday in the Astrophysical Journal Letters tracked 3I/ATLAS across eight months with JWST's NIRSpec and found its water ice locked in micron-scale grains heavily mixed with rocky dust — a spectrum closer to our trans-Neptunian objects than to typical comets. The first sea-going amphibian in the fossil record surfaces in Guizhou

Why it matters: Gerrothorax maritimus, a roughly 237-million-year-old armored stem amphibian described Monday in Nature Ecology & Evolution from two nearly complete Chinese skeletons, lived entirely in the sea — something no living amphibian can do, because their skin can't handle salt. Strontium isotopes back the marine reading, and the authors argue its full dermal armor was the workaround; paleontologist Steve Brusatte calls the find potentially revolutionary if it holds.
