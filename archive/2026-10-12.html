Paper ceasefires, real premiums.

The weekend produced two declared de-escalations and zero confirmed ones. Tehran floated reopening the Strait of Hormuz and called Washington's answer insufficient; Trump announced a Russia-Ukraine "energy ceasefire" that Zelenskyy learned about from the press while Kharkiv was being hit. The measurable world kept moving the other way: Brent up 4.4% in five sessions, the ten-year at 5.24%, the Pentagon's war toll raised to 21. And still the tape rallied — a winning week for every major index with the VIX under 15, because the market has picked its story and the story is AI capex: TSMC just printed a record quarter and SpaceX wants to borrow $40 billion to buy GPUs. Keep one eye on the strait and the other on Tuesday's bank earnings; between them sits everything this market believes.

Iran turns the strait into a toll road it can no longer collect

Why it matters: More than a dozen strikes on shipping since late September have kept Brent above $100 — yet oil volumes through Hormuz are rising again as Iran's grip loosens, which is exactly why Tehran is escalating against tankers as far away as Fujairah. A clock that disciplines its own laser

Why it matters: TU Wien and Germany's PTB ran the first thorium-229 nuclear clock with a feedback loop — the nuclei themselves correct the laser's drift — for more than 24 hours unattended, published in Nature with instability near 10⁻¹⁵ over a day.
