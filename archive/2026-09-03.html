Science & Space

Space8/10

NASA’s Roman telescope launches to map dark energy across a billion galaxies

Why it matters: The $4.3 billion Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope lifted off Aug 30 on a Falcon Heavy from Kennedy and is now on a three-month cruise to Sun–Earth L2. Its infrared camera has a field of view 100 times Hubble’s and surveys the sky ~1,000 times faster — built to measure dark energy, take a census of exoplanets, and photograph a billion galaxies.

What’s next: NASA expects Roman’s first images by early 2027.

Earth6/10

A mushroom-shaped superplume is helping tear Africa apart

Why it matters: Using GPS and seismic data, researchers link a vast upwelling of hot mantle rock — the African Superplume — to unusual surface motion along the East African Rift, showing plumes actively deform the crust. The finding sharpens the picture of how continents split and, over tens of millions of years, how a new ocean basin may one day cleave the Horn of Africa from the rest of the continent.
