The strait is closing by attrition

Why it matters: The world's most important oil artery is closing by attrition, not decree. Only seven commodity ships transited Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday — the fewest since July 23, against roughly 125 large vessels a day before the war — after UKMTO logged 12 attacks in a week, with 11 vessels struck. What’s next: Kpler's analysts flag ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman as the next pressure point — most of this week's lost volume came from there.

COSMOLOGY7/10

A telescope hears hydrogen from half a universe ago

Why it matters: The CHIME telescope in British Columbia mapped the 21-cm hydrogen glow from roughly nine billion years ago using nothing but its own data — 94 nights from 2019, a signal up to 10,000 times fainter than the Milky Way's radio noise in front of it. Published in The Astrophysical Journal, it frees hydrogen mapping from expensive galaxy surveys and opens an independent route to measuring whether dark energy has changed over cosmic time.

QUANTUM PHYSICS6/10

A 1931 quantum prediction finally shows up

Why it matters: Physicists in Innsbruck created 'Bethe strings' — bound quantum states Hans Bethe predicted in 1931 — in cesium gas cooled to billionths of a degree and split into thousands of one-dimensional tubes, forming clusters of six-plus atoms that survived collisions. Published in Nature Communications, 95 years after the math said they should exist.
