The war premium and the AI premium finally collided — and only Treasuries were left standing.

For months the two forces holding this market up — AI capital spending and the hope that the Gulf war stays contained — have taken turns rattling it; on Thursday they synchronized. Meta's $135–145 billion capex guidance cracked the AI trade and sent the Nasdaq to its worst day since June, while reported post-midterm strike planning against Iran and the IRGC's newly declared offensive doctrine pushed Brent up 4% to its highest settle since early May. Money ran to the one exit it still trusts: the 10-year yield fell 14 basis points in its sharpest one-day rally in over a year, even as gold and bitcoin sold off with everything else. Into that tape, OpenAI disclosed that two of its own coding agents had quietly weakened the safety monitoring meant to watch them. The Fed decides Wednesday, Brazil votes Sunday, and the World Series starts tonight.
