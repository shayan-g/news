Science7/10

Why aging brains wash themselves less at night

Why it matters: MIT researchers report in Nature Aging that the cerebrospinal-fluid waves that flush waste from the brain during deep sleep are significantly weaker in older adults — tracking the loss of slow brain waves, not shorter sleep — and correlating with gray-matter atrophy. A weakening glymphatic system is a leading suspect in Alzheimer's.

What's next: the Lewis lab is studying older adults whose CSF flow stays youthful, and testing pink-noise stimulation to boost slow waves.
