THE PRINT9/10

No disinflation in sight: CPI rises 0.4% in a month, and the hike is all but priced

Why it matters: The August CPI, released this morning, rose 0.4% on the month — four times July’s 0.1% — holding annual inflation at 3.4% while core came in at 2.4%, its monthly gain a tenth above forecast. It was the last major inflation reading before Wednesday’s FOMC decision, and it did not deliver the slowdown Chair Kevin Warsh had signaled was needed: Nationwide’s chief economist moved her call to a quarter-point hike, and futures now price one at better than 70%, up from roughly 60% a day earlier.

What’s next: FOMC decision Wednesday, September 16 — with the S&P down four straight sessions into it.

THE STRAIT8/10

Ten ships in one wave — and 42 tankers now sit waiting outside Hormuz

Why it matters: Iran said it attacked ten vessels near the strait — the largest declared wave of shipping attacks by either side since the war began, per Reuters — and UKMTO reported more merchant ships hit by disabling fire overnight in the northern Gulf and Gulf of Oman. MarineTraffic counts 42 tankers waiting to transit, hundreds more loitering in the wider Gulf, and analysts now describe the waterway as an environmental tinderbox: every drifting, holed hull is a spill that hasn’t happened yet.

THE FALLOUT7/10

The war’s climate bill: coal heads for a record 8.94 billion tonnes

Why it matters: With Hormuz LNG shipments choked, the IEA now projects global coal use will rise 1.2% to a record ~8.94 billion tonnes in 2026 — a total that was forecast to fall before the war. China, India, Japan, South Korea and even Europe are running coal plants harder to replace priced-out gas; the US, where gas stays cheap, is the exception at −7%.

What’s next: The IEA says coal could fall back in 2027 — if the strait reopens and LNG flows recover.

TECH & AI8/10

The $20 billion deal that wasn’t a merger: DOJ opens a file on Nvidia–Groq

Why it matters: The Justice Department is investigating whether Nvidia structured its $20 billion non-exclusive license for Groq’s inference-chip technology — which also brought founder Jonathan Ross and several executives to Nvidia — to function as an acquisition while dodging antitrust review, and has sent a formal request for information. Officials reportedly don’t expect an unwind; the harder question is whether the AI era’s signature deal shape — license the tech, hire the team, buy nothing — survives contact with merger law.

What’s next: A fine is possible; a template ruling on AI-era ‘non-acquisitions’ would matter far more.

Science & Space

SCIENCE8/10

1,700 supernovae vs. dark energy: a new analysis says the acceleration may be an illusion

Why it matters: A Tata Institute–Oxford team led by Subir Sarkar reanalyzed more than 1,700 Type Ia supernovae and reports that once you correct for the age of the stars that produce them, the evidence for accelerating expansion weakens — and the apparent acceleration varies with direction, which the standard dark-energy picture can’t easily do. A rebuttal in MNRAS says these effects are already accounted for. Two-thirds of the universe’s energy budget is riding on who is right.

What’s next: Rubin Observatory’s LSST will deliver hundreds of thousands of supernovae — the arbiter both camps accept.

SCIENCE7/10

Three black holes that shouldn’t have partners: Gaia’s quiet trio breaks the binary rulebook

Why it matters: Gaia’s astrometry has revealed three dormant stellar-mass black holes through the wobble of small companion stars in orbits of a hundred to a few thousand days. Two companions orbit close enough that the black hole’s giant progenitor should have engulfed them before it collapsed — so either binaries evolve in ways the models miss, or these systems formed by capture.

SCIENCE6/10

MeerKAT hears whispers of hydrogen from 4.8 billion light-years out

Why it matters: Astronomers at Manchester and the University of the Western Cape used South Africa’s MeerKAT array to directly detect the 21-centimetre hum of neutral hydrogen at distances of 3.67 and 4.76 billion light-years — published in ApJ Letters, and a working proof of intensity mapping: charting the cosmos by hydrogen’s combined glow instead of counting galaxies one by one. It’s a cheap, fast way to map the dark-matter web at scales galaxy surveys can’t afford.
