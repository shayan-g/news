Security 8/10

Eight CA keys that could impersonate the web, cracked in milliseconds

Why it matters: A scripting error in an April 2025 ceremony left eight eMudhra intermediate certificate-authority keys (six TLS, two code-signing) with trivially weak P-384 private keys. A researcher reported it Friday at 18:05 UTC; Chrome had a block out by 19:45 and Mozilla by 22:59 — and Certificate Transparency logs show no malicious certs issued. The long tail is everything that isn't a browser: Go shipped CVE fixes, and OS and language trust stores still need updates.

What's next: Chrome removes the emSign ECC Root G3 from its root store on October 27.

Science — lead 6/10

The "mouth of hell" is hiding a second magma chamber

Why it matters: Penn State researchers tracking Nicaragua's Masaya volcano with Sentinel-1 satellite radar (2018–2024) found two active magma reservoirs — a central chamber that deflated ~3 cm then re-inflated, plus a previously unrecognized shallow source only ~200 m beneath the Santiago crater. Two million people live within about 20 km; the authors see no immediate danger, but hazard models just got a second variable.

Paleontology 6/10

A Jurassic bird that never flew rewrites flight's first chapter

Why it matters: Yuezhuornis yangyuanensis, a ~150-million-year-old fossil from Fujian, pairs bird hips and hindlimbs with serrated teeth and a sickle claw — and its limb proportions match ground-dwelling birds across 377 living species. If some of the earliest birds never flew, flightlessness wasn't only a later, secondary adaptation — an assumption that goes back to Darwin. One caveat: a single, incomplete specimen.
